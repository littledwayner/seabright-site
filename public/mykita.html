A former nursery, and a very Berlin beginning.

Mykita began in 2003, when a group of friends started a company in a former children’s day nursery in Berlin. The name nods to it: Kita, the German word for the place. It left a lasting mark on the house, a culture of curiosity and play.

Today the work happens under one roof in Kreuzberg, at Mykita Haus. Design, development and hand assembly sit side by side, so an idea can be watched becoming a frame.

Steel, shaped without screws.

The first collection arrived in 2004: stainless steel frames cut from flat sheet and folded into form, assembled without welds or screws. It was a quiet technical decision, and the same precision runs through the house today.

The result is a frame that sits lightly on the face and feels exact in the hand, with nothing added for show.
